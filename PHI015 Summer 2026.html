Description

This course introduces and examines ethical issues related to health care and medical research. Some of the questions we will examine are: Should doctors ever act contrary to the will of the patient? How should decisions be made for patients without decision-making capacity? How should we balance individual liberty and public health, particularly amidst a pandemic? What is the just way to distribute health care resources? Is abortion morally defensible? Should doctors be allowed to assist patients in dying? Should genetic enhancement of human beings be permitted?

These issues are extremely important for two reasons. First, many of them are directly relevant to everyday medical practices. Second, they also raise profound theoretical questions, such as the nature of death and dying, the meaning of free will, and more. If you are interested in the course for either of these reasons, or both, this is the class for you. Over the next five weeks, we will explore these important topics together.

Assessment

  1. In-class activities (30%): There will be six in-class assignments during weeks 2, 3, 4, and 6. To complete these assignments, students will first discuss the relevant philosophical issues in small groups. Then, each student will complete an individual in-class writing piece.
  2. In-class Midterm Exam (20%): The Midterm Exam will be a closed-book, in-class, written exam. It will cover materials from weeks 1 to 3.
  3. In-class Paper (20%): There is one major writing assignment for this class. It will be written in class. Students should purchase one Bluebook from the UC Davis bookstore for the essay.
  4. Take-home Essay Revision/Reflection (10%): Students may either revise the essay they wrote in class or submit a reflection report on that essay.
  5. Take-home Final Exam (20%): The Final Exam will be a closed-book take-home exam.

Course Policies

Accessibility: Accommodations are available for students with needs. Please contact the UC Davis Student Disability Center within the first week of the summer session if you need accommodations.

Late Work: Late submissions for in-class assignments are not accepted unless you email me in advance with a legitimate reason. For Essay Revision / Reflection Assignment, late submissions will be penalized 5% for each day late.

Academic Integrity: The Code of Academic Conduct is central to the values of our scholarly community. Violations of academic integrity are taken seriously and will be handled according to university policy.

AI Use Policy: The use of generative AI tools to generate your writings is strictly prohibited in this class.
